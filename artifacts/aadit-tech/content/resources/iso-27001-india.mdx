ISO 27001 certification in India is an independent assessment of an organisation's information security management system, or ISMS, against ISO/IEC 27001. The standard is international: an Indian organisation follows the same management-system requirements, while addressing the legal, contractual and operational context relevant to its own business.

Certification is not a product purchased from a consultant. Your organisation owns and operates the ISMS; a consultant can support readiness; an independent certification body decides whether certification requirements have been met. [Aadit's ISO 27001 consulting service](/compliance/iso-27001) supports preparation and audit readiness, rather than issuing certificates.

## Start with the customer requirement and scope

Ask why certification is needed. An enterprise vendor review may require a certificate covering a particular SaaS service. A tender may specify an accredited certificate, named locations or a particular standard edition. Record the requirement before choosing a boundary that is convenient but commercially irrelevant.

Scope identifies the activities, people, locations, technology and interfaces covered by the ISMS. Include relevant dependencies: a cloud-hosted application still depends on identity, engineering access, suppliers and incident handling. Outsourcing infrastructure does not outsource your responsibility to manage the associated risks.

Check that remote workers, shared services and outsourced processes are represented appropriately. If some activities are outside the scope, explain the boundary and the controls governing interactions with them. A certificate with a limited scope should not be presented as assurance over the entire group or every product.

## The certification process

### 1. Assess gaps and assign responsibility

Compare current practices with the standard's requirements and identify missing controls or evidence. Assign a programme owner with management support and authority to coordinate engineering, IT, HR, legal and procurement. Each action needs an accountable person and a realistic completion date.

Distinguish between missing documentation and missing operation. An access-review policy does not demonstrate that access was reviewed, and a backup schedule does not prove that recovery works. A gap assessment should identify both problems rather than treating certification as a document-writing exercise.

### 2. Assess risks and plan treatment

Establish a consistent risk assessment method and use it to evaluate the information your organisation needs to protect. Identify relevant threats, existing controls, consequences and treatment decisions. Record risk owners and how residual risks are accepted.

Prepare the Statement of Applicability to explain the controls considered necessary, their implementation status and the rationale for inclusion or exclusion. ISO/IEC 27001:2022 contains 93 Annex A reference controls; the management-system clauses also matter. Selecting controls is a risk-based exercise, not a decision to buy every listed technology.

### 3. Implement and operate the ISMS

Implement policies and processes alongside the technical work that makes them effective. Depending on scope, evidence may include access reviews, joiner and leaver records, supplier assessments, vulnerability handling, restore tests, security awareness and incident exercises.

Keep evidence attributable and dated, with appropriate access restrictions. Existing IT and security systems may already produce useful records; validate that they actually demonstrate the control you are claiming. [VAPT reporting](/resources/vapt-report-sample) can support technical risk management, but a test report alone is not an ISMS.

### 4. Complete internal audit and management review

Internal audit evaluates whether the ISMS conforms to planned requirements and operates effectively. Plan an audit that covers the relevant scope and preserves auditor objectivity. Document results, corrective actions and how their effectiveness is checked.

Management review is a leadership activity, not simply a signature on the policy pack. Review performance, audit findings, changing risks, resources and improvement opportunities. Record decisions and follow through on them before presenting the programme for external assessment.

### 5. Undergo Stage 1 and Stage 2

Stage 1 assesses readiness for the next stage, including the management-system documentation and relevant preparation. Use any findings to address gaps rather than assuming that a booked Stage 2 date proves readiness.

Stage 2 evaluates implementation and effectiveness within the agreed scope. The certification body examines evidence and may interview control owners. It determines the treatment of nonconformities and the certification decision under its process. Consulting support cannot guarantee the outcome or remove the need for independent assessment.

## Choosing a certification body in India

Verify accreditation for ISMS certification rather than relying on an “ISO certified” logo on a sales page. Check the certification body's entry with its accreditation body and confirm that the relevant standard is covered. In India, [NABCB](https://nabcb.qci.org.in/) provides accreditation information; bodies accredited elsewhere should be checked through the relevant authority.

Ask how your proposed scope, workforce, sites and operating complexity affect audit planning. Confirm auditor competence, availability, certificate verification and the process for handling findings. Independence matters: preparation and certification should not be sold as the same unqualified promise.

Compare written proposals on the same basis. Separate certification-body fees from consultant fees, technical remediation and internal effort. Our [ISO 27001 cost guide](/compliance/iso-27001/cost) provides a budgeting checklist without presenting an unverified India-wide price.

## Indian legal and regulatory context

ISO 27001 helps organise security governance, but it does not replace legal analysis. Map the obligations relevant to your organisation, including customer contracts, privacy requirements and applicable sector rules. Financial services, healthcare and service providers may have different duties even when they use the same standard.

Treat [India's DPDP Act](/compliance/dpdp-act), CERT-In directions and relevant RBI or SEBI requirements as separate sources to review where applicable. Do not assume a certificate proves compliance with every provision. Implementation and commencement details can change, so use current official materials and appropriate legal or regulatory advice.

For companies selling internationally, confirm which assurance the buyer accepts. [SOC 2 and ISO 27001](/compare/soc-2-vs-iso-27001) have overlapping control themes but produce different outputs. A customer asking for a SOC 2 report may not accept an ISO certificate as a substitute.

## Evidence to prepare before the audit

| Area | Examples to organise |
| --- | --- |
| Governance | Scope, responsibilities, approved policies, objectives and management-review records |
| Risk | Assessment method, risk register, treatment plan and Statement of Applicability |
| Operations | Access reviews, change records, supplier assessments and incident records |
| Technical assurance | Vulnerability handling, testing evidence and recovery verification relevant to the scope |
| Improvement | Internal-audit results, corrective actions and effectiveness checks |

This is a planning guide, not an exhaustive audit checklist. Tailor the evidence to the actual requirements and controls in your ISMS. Avoid collecting sensitive records into an unrestricted shared folder simply to make an audit easier.

## After certification

Keep the management system operating between external assessments. Review risks when services, suppliers or locations change; maintain evidence; and resolve issues through the corrective-action process. Plan for the certification body's surveillance and recertification activities rather than treating the first certificate as the end of the programme.

Confirm certificate validity and scope when sharing it with customers. If the business changes materially, discuss the implications with the certification body. A certificate is evidence of an assessed management system within a stated scope, not a guarantee that a breach can never happen.

## Frequently asked questions

### How does a company get ISO 27001 certified in India?

Define the ISMS scope, assess information security risks, implement and operate the management system, and complete internal audit and management review. An independent certification body then conducts Stage 1 and Stage 2 audits. Certification depends on its assessment and the resolution of relevant nonconformities, not on buying a consulting package.

### Does ISO issue the certificate?

No. ISO develops standards but does not perform certification or issue ISO 27001 certificates. An independent certification body assesses the organisation. Check the body's accreditation for the relevant standard, the proposed certificate scope and the verification route before appointing it.

### Is ISO 27001 mandatory for every Indian company?

No blanket requirement makes ISO 27001 certification mandatory for every Indian company. A customer contract, tender or sector-specific obligation may require it. Review the requirements that apply to your organisation separately; certification does not replace compliance with Indian privacy law or sector regulations.

### How long does certification take?

Timing depends on the ISMS scope, existing controls, remediation work, available staff and audit scheduling. The organisation must demonstrate an operating management system, not only completed documents. Build a schedule after a gap assessment and confirm audit availability with the certification body rather than relying on a universal deadline.

### Does certification cover every company location and product?

Only the activities, locations and services stated in the certificate's scope are covered. Dependencies outside that boundary still need to be considered in the ISMS. Make sure the proposed scope corresponds to the service your customer buys; a narrow certificate may not answer a broader vendor-review requirement.
