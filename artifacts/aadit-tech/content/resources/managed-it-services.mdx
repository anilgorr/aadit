Managed IT services means outsourcing the day-to-day running of an organisation’s IT — monitoring, user support, patching, account and device management, backup and infrastructure operations — to a provider under an ongoing agreement with defined service levels, rather than paying for help each time something breaks.

The defining characteristic is the commercial model. A managed service provider is paid a recurring fee to keep systems working, which means their incentive is to prevent problems. A break-fix provider is paid when something fails, which means their incentive runs the other way. That difference shapes everything about how the two operate.

## What is included

Scope varies by agreement, but a full managed IT service usually covers:

- Monitoring — infrastructure health, application availability, backup success, certificate and licence expiry, with alerting to the provider rather than to you

- Help desk — user support across devices, applications, access and connectivity, within defined response targets

- Patch and update management — operating systems, applications and firmware on a schedule, with testing before deployment

- Identity and device management — account provisioning and de-provisioning, access reviews, device enrolment and configuration

- Backup and recovery — backup operation, monitoring, and periodic restore testing

- Infrastructure operations — servers, networks and cloud resources, including capacity management

- Vendor coordination — dealing with software and hardware vendors on your behalf

- Reporting — service level performance, ticket volumes and trends, risks and recommendations

Security monitoring is a separate discipline. Some providers include basic security hygiene; detection and response belongs with a security operations centre, not a help desk.

## How managed IT differs from the alternatives

| Aspect | Break-fix | Staff augmentation | Managed IT services |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Commercial model | Per incident or hourly | Per person per month | Recurring fee for defined scope |
| Provider’s incentive | More incidents | Longer placement | Fewer incidents |
| Coverage | When you call | Working hours of the individual | Defined, often 24/7 |
| Accountability | For the ticket | For the hours worked | For the service level |
| Best for | Very small environments | Filling a specific skills gap | Ongoing operations at scale |

## Who needs managed IT services

- Organisations that need coverage outside office hours but cannot justify staffing shifts internally

- Growing companies whose internal IT spends more time on operations than on work that moves the business

- Companies with compliance obligations that require evidenced patching, access control and backup testing

- Distributed organisations where users across multiple sites or countries need consistent support

- Companies in transition — post-acquisition, mid-migration, or between internal IT leaders

## How managed IT services are priced

Most agreements price per user or per device per month, sometimes with a separate charge for servers and infrastructure. Watch for what sits outside the recurring fee: project work, onboarding, after-hours support if the base tier is business hours only, and on-site visits. A per-user price is only comparable between providers when the exclusions are the same.

## What to check before signing

- Service levels — response and resolution targets by severity, and what happens when they are missed

- Coverage hours — whether 24/7 means a staffed desk or an on-call engineer

- Escalation path — who you reach at 2am and how quickly

- Security of their access — a provider holds privileged access to your environment; ask how that access is controlled, logged and reviewed

- Reporting — what you receive monthly, and whether it is useful to leadership

- Exit terms — how documentation, credentials and data come back to you

## Common misconceptions

- “It means replacing our IT team.” The usual model is co-managed: the provider runs monitoring, help desk and routine operations; the internal team keeps architecture, vendor strategy and business-specific work.

- “It’s only for companies without IT staff.” Organisations with substantial internal teams use managed services precisely to free those teams from operational load.

- “It covers security.” Managed IT and managed security are different services with different skills. Assume security monitoring is excluded unless the agreement says otherwise.

- “The cheapest per-user price wins.” Per-user pricing is only comparable when scope, coverage hours and exclusions match. They rarely do.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is included in managed IT services?

Typically monitoring, help desk support, patch management, identity and device management, backup operation and testing, infrastructure operations, vendor coordination and reporting — all under defined service levels for a recurring fee.

### What is the difference between managed IT services and break-fix support?

Break-fix charges per incident, so the provider earns more when things go wrong. Managed services charge a recurring fee for a defined scope, so the provider earns more when things keep working. Coverage, accountability and reporting differ accordingly.

### How are managed IT services priced?

Usually per user or per device per month, sometimes with separate charges for servers and infrastructure. Project work, onboarding and after-hours support may sit outside the recurring fee, so compare exclusions rather than headline rates.

### Do managed IT services include cybersecurity?

Not by default. Managed IT covers operations; security monitoring and incident response are a separate discipline delivered by a security operations centre. Some providers offer both, but confirm what the agreement actually includes.

### Will we lose control of our IT?

No, if the agreement is structured properly. You retain ownership of systems, data and decisions. The provider operates within an agreed scope and reports against it. Exit terms covering documentation and credential handover are what protect this in practice.
